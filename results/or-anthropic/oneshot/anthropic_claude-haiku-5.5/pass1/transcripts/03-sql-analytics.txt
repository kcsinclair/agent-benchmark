Here are the four queries.

**q1.sql** — Revenue per customer (completed orders only)

```sql
SELECT
  c.name AS customer_name,
  ROUND(SUM(oi.quantity * p.unit_price), 2) AS revenue
FROM customers c
JOIN orders o       ON o.customer_id = c.id AND o.status = 'completed'
JOIN order_items oi ON oi.order_id = o.id
JOIN products p     ON p.id = oi.product_id
GROUP BY c.id, c.name
ORDER BY revenue DESC, customer_name ASC;
```

**q2.sql** — Top 3 products by units sold (completed orders only)

```sql
SELECT
  p.name AS product_name,
  SUM(oi.quantity) AS units_sold
FROM products p
JOIN order_items oi ON oi.product_id = p.id
JOIN orders o       ON o.id = oi.order_id AND o.status = 'completed'
GROUP BY p.id, p.name
ORDER BY units_sold DESC, product_name ASC
LIMIT 3;
```

**q3.sql** — Customers with no orders of any status

```sql
SELECT
  c.name AS customer_name,
  c.country AS country
FROM customers c
WHERE NOT EXISTS (
  SELECT 1 FROM orders o WHERE o.customer_id = c.id
)
ORDER BY customer_name ASC;
```

**q4.sql** — Monthly revenue for 2025 (completed orders, non-zero months only)

```sql
SELECT
  strftime('%Y-%m', o.ordered_at) AS month,
  ROUND(SUM(oi.quantity * p.unit_price), 2) AS revenue
FROM orders o
JOIN order_items oi ON oi.order_id = o.id
JOIN products p     ON p.id = oi.product_id
WHERE o.status = 'completed'
  AND strftime('%Y', o.ordered_at) = '2025'
GROUP BY month
HAVING SUM(oi.quantity * p.unit_price) <> 0
ORDER BY month ASC;
```

Notes on the design:
- q1 and q2 filter `status = 'completed'` in the join condition, so non-completed orders never contribute, and customers/products without completed sales drop out naturally via the inner joins.
- q3 uses `NOT EXISTS` rather than a `LEFT JOIN ... IS NULL`, which avoids any ambiguity and is robust to customers with orders in any status.
- q4 uses `strftime('%Y', ...)` to restrict to 2025 and a `HAVING` clause to exclude zero-revenue months.
